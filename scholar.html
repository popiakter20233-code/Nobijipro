Scholar Review

A short orientation for reviewing scholars: what the system is, how flags work, how to return corrections.

1. What you are reviewing

Each chapter draft is paired with a scholar packet that contains: a one-paragraph chapter summary; a claim summary; the full citation block for every HID; disputed points and weak narrations encountered; AI-assisted translations needing approval; open questions specifically for you; and a blank response template at the end.

You do not need to write your own document. Fill in §10 of the packet — that is the deliverable.

2. The HID system

Every narration the project uses has a stable identifier: HID-NNNN. The single canonical record lives in references/hadith_index.md. Chapters cite by HID; one narration → one HID, used across the whole book consistently.

3. The flags — what they mean

Inline next to every citation you will see one or more flags in [BRACKETS]. They are features, not noise.

  • NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW — Default on draft entries; awaiting your sign-off.
  • HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED — The translation is AI-assisted; please approve, replace, or specify a published translator.
  • TRANSLATION REVIEW — Translation has reasonable AI default but needs your eye.
  • TRANSLATION DEFERRED — A fiqh-loaded Arabic term (wājib, farḍ, etc.) is preserved in Arabic. Please specify English and Bangla wording from scratch.
  • FIQH REVIEW — The narration carries ruling-form language. Please confirm the chapter avoids implicit fatwa.
  • HISTORICAL REVIEW — Tier-3 source (Shamail / seerah-leaning) used as primary. Please confirm framing.
  • GRADE DISPUTED — Graders disagree; please indicate inclusion proportionality.
  • NAME PROVISIONAL — A glossary entry awaits your canonical-form confirmation.
  • CONTEXT REVIEW REQUIRED — The narration is bound to a specific event; please confirm general application.

4. How to return your corrections

The packet's response template (§10) is your workspace. Per claim:

  • Verdict: APPROVED / APPROVED WITH EDITS / NEEDS REWORK / REJECT.
  • Edits and additions: the wording you would prefer.
  • Translation approvals or replacements: per HID, the wording or named published translator.
  • Canonical-name decisions: lock as suggested or substitute.
  • Overall verdict, signature, date, affiliation.

Save your response in scholar_review/feedback/<chapter>.feedback.md, or hand it back in any format and the editorial team will transcribe.

5. Approval workflow

After approval, the chapter moves to scholar_review/approved/; HIDs you approved become eligible for HIGH confidence; cleared tags drop from citations on the next render. After "needs rework," the chapter returns to drafts/; flagged sections are revised; the packet is re-prepared with your prior feedback referenced.

6. The confidence ceiling

Every HID currently sits at MEDIUM confidence. This is not an admission of weakness — it is the system honestly reporting the bound of its own checking. The authenticity gate caps confidence at MEDIUM until Arabic wording is verified against a published edition. Your scholar approval, paired with a translator lock, is what allows promotion to HIGH.

❦

What you absolutely should not be asked to do

If you ever receive a packet that asks you to approve a fabricated narration, a translation that elides the meaning of a fiqh term, a chapter framing that issues fatwa under cover of description, or a chapter that quietly drops NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW tags — please refuse and notify the project. The system is built to make those asks impossible, but no system is perfect.

A smaller, fully-verified book is better than a larger book with even one fabricated narration. Your time is gratefully received.
