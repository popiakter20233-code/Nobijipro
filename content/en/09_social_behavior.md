# Social Behavior

> *Draft status:* MEDIUM. Not yet promoted to `verified/`.

---

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught the Sahabah رضي الله عنهم how to be among people. The narrations preserve two paired teachings — *what is loved* and *what is to be guarded against*. Mercy is named as the standard, gentleness as the divine attribute that draws response; anger is named as the most natural failure of self, and the strongest man as the one who masters it. This chapter walks through both in turn.

---

# Part 1 — Mercy and Gentleness

## Allah is gentle and loves gentleness

> **Arabic:** عَنْ عَائِشَةَ رضي الله عنها عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: «إِنَّ اللَّهَ رَفِيقٌ يُحِبُّ الرِّفْقَ، وَيُعْطِي عَلَى الرِّفْقِ مَا لَا يُعْطِي عَلَى الْعُنْفِ، وَمَا لَا يُعْطِي عَلَى مَا سِوَاهُ»
>
> **English:** *"Indeed, Allah is gentle and loves gentleness; He gives for gentleness what He does not give for harshness, nor for anything else."*
>
> *— Aishah رضي الله عنها. Sahih Muslim, no. 2593. See HID-0034. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

The Prophet ﷺ ties Divine attribute to Divine response. Allah ﷻ is gentle, and He gives for gentleness what He gives for nothing else. The framing presents *rifq* — gentleness — as an investment with a specific, named return.

## Mercy on the young, respect for the elders

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَنَسٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: «لَيْسَ مِنَّا مَنْ لَمْ يَرْحَمْ صَغِيرَنَا، وَيُوَقِّرْ كَبِيرَنَا»
>
> **English:** *"He is not of us who does not show mercy to our young and respect to our elders."*
>
> *— Anas ibn Malik رضي الله عنه. Jami` at-Tirmidhi, no. 1919. See HID-0035. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

Two duties, one phrase: *yarḥam* (show mercy) for the small, *yuwaqqir* (give due respect) for the great. The Prophet's ﷺ formulation excludes the indifferent.

## Faith requires fellow-feeling

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَنَسٍ رضي الله عنه عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: «لَا يُؤْمِنُ أَحَدُكُمْ حَتَّى يُحِبَّ لِأَخِيهِ مَا يُحِبُّ لِنَفْسِهِ»
>
> **English:** *"None of you truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself."*
>
> *— Anas ibn Malik رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 13; Sahih Muslim, no. 45 (muttafaq alayh). See HID-0036. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

The Prophet ﷺ ties social mercy to *iman* itself. The standard is precise — what one wants for oneself one wants for one's brother — and the consequence is named: faith is incomplete without it.

# Part 2 — Anger and Forgiveness

If mercy is what is loved, anger is what is to be guarded against. The Prophet ﷺ named it as the moment in which a man most easily loses what he is.

## Strength is self-control

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: «لَيْسَ الشَّدِيدُ بِالصُّرَعَةِ، إِنَّمَا الشَّدِيدُ الَّذِي يَمْلِكُ نَفْسَهُ عِنْدَ الْغَضَبِ»
>
> **English:** *"The strong is not the one who throws people down. The strong is the one who controls himself when angry."*
>
> *— Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 6114; Sahih Muslim, no. 2609 (muttafaq alayh). See HID-0037. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

The Prophet ﷺ redefines a common word. *Shadīd* — strong — was understood as physical strength; he ﷺ relocates it inward. Strength is what survives the moment of greatest temptation to lose it.

## The repeated counsel

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ رَجُلًا قَالَ لِلنَّبِيِّ ﷺ: أَوْصِنِي. قَالَ: «لَا تَغْضَبْ» فَرَدَّدَ مِرَارًا، قَالَ: «لَا تَغْضَبْ»
>
> **English:** *"A man said to the Prophet ﷺ: 'Counsel me.' He said: 'Do not become angry.' The man repeated his request several times, and each time the Prophet ﷺ said: 'Do not become angry.'"*
>
> *— Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 6116. See HID-0038. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

When asked for counsel and asked again, the Prophet ﷺ gives the same two words. The repetition is the answer: this is not a small matter.

## The remedy — seeking refuge

> **Arabic:** عَنْ سُلَيْمَانَ بْنِ صُرَدٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: اسْتَبَّ رَجُلَانِ عِنْدَ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ، فَجَعَلَ أَحَدُهُمَا يَغْضَبُ ويَحْمَرُّ وَجْهُهُ. فَقَالَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ: «إِنِّي لَأَعْلَمُ كَلِمَةً لَوْ قَالَهَا لَذَهَبَ عَنْهُ مَا يَجِدُ — أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ»
>
> **English:** *"Two men quarrelled in the presence of the Prophet ﷺ. One was becoming angry, his face turning red. The Prophet ﷺ said: 'I know a phrase that if he said it, what he is feeling would leave him — A'udhu billahi min ash-shaytan ir-rajim (I seek refuge in Allah from the accursed Satan).'"*
>
> *— Sulayman ibn Surad رضي الله عنه. Sunan Abu Dawud, no. 4782. See HID-0039. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED] [NAME PROVISIONAL]*

The Prophet ﷺ describes anger as a state with a remedy. He ﷺ does not silence the angry man; he ﷺ offers him a phrase that turns the moment toward Allah ﷻ.

## Practical lessons
- The Prophet ﷺ described Allah ﷻ as gentle, loving gentleness, and giving for it what He gives for nothing else (HID-0034).
- He ﷺ excluded from his ﷺ community those who do not show mercy to the young and respect to the elderly (HID-0035).
- He ﷺ tied complete faith to wanting for one's brother what one wants for oneself (HID-0036).
- He ﷺ named true strength as self-control at the moment of anger (HID-0037).
- He ﷺ repeatedly counselled "do not become angry" (HID-0038).
- He ﷺ named *A'udhu billahi min ash-shaytan ir-rajim* as a phrase that removes anger (HID-0039).
