# Clothing and Appearance

> *Draft status:* MEDIUM. HID-0020 carries `[FIQH REVIEW]` (an instruction). Apparent contradiction between HID-0019 and HID-0021 reconciled in §5; scholar confirmation requested. Not yet promoted to `verified/`.

---

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ dressed simply. The narrations preserve concrete details — the *cut* of his ﷺ preferred garment, the *colour* he ﷺ recommended for daily wear, and the *cloth* he ﷺ liked best to wear. Two narrations describing the "most beloved garment" appear at first glance to differ; this chapter presents them side by side, with the standard scholarly reconciliation, for the scholar's confirmation.

---

## The most beloved cut — the *qamīṣ*

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أُمِّ سَلَمَةَ رضي الله عنها قَالَتْ: «كَانَ أَحَبَّ الثِّيَابِ إِلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ الْقَمِيصُ»
>
> **English:** *"The most beloved garment to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was the qamīṣ (long shirt)."*
>
> *— Umm Salamah رضي الله عنها. Jami` at-Tirmidhi, no. 1762. See HID-0019. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED] [NAME PROVISIONAL] [TERM PROVISIONAL — qamīṣ]*

Umm Salamah رضي الله عنها names a *style* — the *qamīṣ*, a long shirt-like garment worn over the body.

---

## The recommended colour — white

> **Arabic:** عَنِ ابْنِ عَبَّاسٍ رضي الله عنهما قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: «الْبَسُوا مِنْ ثِيَابِكُمُ الْبَيَاضَ، فَإِنَّهَا مِنْ خَيْرِ ثِيَابِكُمْ، وَكَفِّنُوا فِيهَا مَوْتَاكُمْ»
>
> **English:** *"Wear white among your garments, for they are among your best garments; and shroud your dead in them."*
>
> *— Ibn Abbas رضي الله عنهما. Jami` at-Tirmidhi, no. 994; Sunan Abu Dawud, no. 3878; Sunan Ibn Majah, no. 1472. See HID-0020. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED] [FIQH REVIEW]*

The narration is in three Sunan and Musnad Ahmad — multi-source corroborated. The Prophet ﷺ both *recommends* white for daily wear and *links* it to the practice of shrouding (*kafan*). The same cloth that clothes the living and the dead.

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَنَسٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: «كَانَ أَحَبَّ الثِّيَابِ إِلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ أَنْ يَلْبَسَهَا الْحِبَرَةَ»

>
> *— Anas ibn Malik رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 5810; Sahih Muslim, no. 2080 (muttafaq alayh). See HID-0021. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED] [NAME PROVISIONAL] [TERM PROVISIONAL — ḥibarah]*

Anas رضي الله عنه names a *cloth* — the *ḥibarah*, a striped Yemeni fabric.

## The right-side principle

Behind the specific garments the Prophet ﷺ chose stands a broader pattern: he ﷺ began clean and noble matters from the right. Aishah رضي الله عنها preserved the principle in a single hadith, and Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه preserved its concrete form for footwear.

### A — Direct hadith evidence

> **Arabic:** عَنْ عَائِشَةَ رضي الله عنها قَالَتْ: «كَانَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ يُعْجِبُهُ التَّيَمُّنُ فِي تَنَعُّلِهِ، وَتَرَجُّلِهِ، وَطُهُورِهِ، وَفِي شَأْنِهِ كُلِّهِ»
>
> **English:** *"The Prophet ﷺ used to love starting from the right side when putting on his shoes, combing his hair, performing his ablution, and in all his affairs."*
>
> *— Aishah رضي الله عنها. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 168; Sahih Muslim, no. 268 (muttafaq alayh). See HID-0052. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

Aishah رضي الله عنها names four specific domains — shoes, hair, wudu, all affairs — and adds the closing phrase *fī shaʾnihi kullihi*: "in all his affairs." That last clause is the textual hook by which the principle extends naturally to other clean acts.

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: «إِذَا انْتَعَلَ أَحَدُكُمْ فَلْيَبْدَأْ بِالْيَمِينِ، وَإِذَا نَزَعَ فَلْيَبْدَأْ بِالشِّمَالِ، لِتَكُنِ الْيُمْنَى أَوَّلَهُمَا تُنْعَلُ، وَآخِرَهُمَا تُنْزَعُ»
>
> **English:** *"When one of you puts on shoes, let him begin with the right; and when he takes them off, let him begin with the left, so that the right is the first to be put on and the last to be taken off."*
>
> *— Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 5856; Sahih Muslim, no. 2097 (muttafaq alayh). See HID-0053. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه gives the principle its concrete shape for footwear: right first when putting on, left first when removing — so that the right is *first in honor and last in removal*.

### B — Scholarly / practical application

The Sahabah and later scholars applied the closing phrase *"in all his affairs"* to clean and noble matters generally. This includes acts not specifically named in the hadith — putting on a *qamīṣ*, entering one's home, entering the masjid, beginning a meal, accepting a gift, and similar. **These extensions are practical applications of the broader principle, not directly stated Sunnahs in their own right.** The chapter labels them as such.

The principle inverts for matters that are unclean or undesirable: the *left* takes precedence in those domains (removing soiled clothing, entering a place of impurity). HID-0053 itself models this pattern — pairing right-on with left-off.

### C — Modern examples

> Although buttons and modern panjabi-style garments are not directly mentioned in the hadith wording, the general Sunnah of preferring the right side in noble and clean matters may be applied by starting with the right sleeve or right side when dressing.

The same inference covers modern button shirts, thobes, jubbas, jackets, and any garment whose form admits a right-or-left start. **What the chapter does not say:** that the Prophet ﷺ buttoned from the right; that he ﷺ wore panjabi; that any specific modern dressing method is itself Sunnah. The Sunnah is the principle in HID-0052; the modern application is the practical extension of that principle.

## §5. A note on the two "most beloved" narrations

Each of the two narrations describes the Prophet's ﷺ "most beloved garment" — but with different items. Scholars commonly reconcile this by noting that the two narrations describe *different categories of preference*: HID-0019 names a **cut/style** (long shirt), while HID-0021 names a **material/origin** (Yemeni striped cloth). On this reading, the Prophet ﷺ preferred long-shirt-style garments cut in the *qamīṣ* form, and among cloths he ﷺ especially liked the *ḥibarah*.

This reconciliation is presented for scholarly review. The chapter does not commit to it without confirmation.

---

## Practical lessons

- The Prophet ﷺ's preferred garment style was the *qamīṣ* (HID-0019).
- He ﷺ recommended white among one's garments and tied it to the practice of shrouding the dead (HID-0020).
- Among cloths, he ﷺ especially liked the *ḥibarah* (HID-0021).
- He ﷺ loved beginning from the right side in shoes, hair, wudu, and "in all his affairs" (HID-0052).
- He ﷺ taught: right foot first when putting on sandals; left first when removing (HID-0053).
- *(Practical application:)* the right-side principle naturally extends to modern dressing — beginning with the right sleeve or right side when putting on a button shirt, panjabi, thobe, or jacket. This is inference from the broader Sunnah, not a directly-stated practice.
