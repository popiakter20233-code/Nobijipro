# Waking Up

> *Draft status:* MEDIUM. Every cited hadith carries `[NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW]` and `[HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]`. Chapter is in `drafts/`, not yet promoted to `verified/`.

---

A Muslim's day, in the way the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ lived it, did not begin with the day. It began the moment the eyes opened. Before getting out of bed, before light at the window, before any sound from the rest of the house — the first thought turned toward Allah ﷻ. The Sahabah رضي الله عنهم preserved a small set of narrations that describe what the Prophet ﷺ actually did in those first moments. Together they show a careful, settled rhythm — quiet, deliberate, unhurried.

---

## The first words

The waking dua paired with the sleeping dua. As the Prophet ﷺ committed his ﷺ soul to Allah ﷻ before lying down, he ﷺ returned to Him on waking with praise.

> **Arabic:** عَنْ حُذَيْفَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: كَانَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ إِذَا اسْتَيْقَظَ مِنَ اللَّيْلِ قَالَ: «الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَحْيَانَا بَعْدَ مَا أَمَاتَنَا، وَإِلَيْهِ النُّشُورُ»
>
> **English:** *"When the Prophet ﷺ awoke at night, he ﷺ would say: 'Praise be to Allah Who has given us life after He caused us to die, and to Him is the resurrection.'"*
>
> *— Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 6312. See HID-0046. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

Sleep is named by the dua as a small death; waking is named as a return. The first sentence the tongue forms is not a complaint about being woken — it is praise for being alive again.

---

## Wiping sleep, opening to recitation

The Prophet ﷺ did not rush from the bed into the day. Ibn Abbas رضي الله عنهما, who once slept in the household, watched the Prophet ﷺ wake at night and preserved the next thing he ﷺ did.

> **Arabic:** عَنِ ابْنِ عَبَّاسٍ رضي الله عنهما قَالَ: «اسْتَيْقَظَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ فَجَعَلَ يَمْسَحُ النَّوْمَ عَنْ وَجْهِهِ بِيَدِهِ، ثُمَّ قَرَأَ الْعَشْرَ الْآيَاتِ الْخَوَاتِيمَ مِنْ سُورَةِ آلِ عِمْرَانَ»
>
> **English:** *"The Messenger of Allah ﷺ awoke; he ﷺ wiped sleep from his face with his hand, then recited the last ten verses of Surah Aal Imran."*
>
> *— Ibn Abbas رضي الله عنهما. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 183; Sahih Muslim, no. 763. See HID-0056. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

Two small acts in sequence: a hand wiping the residue of sleep, and the closing verses of *Aal Imran* — *Inna fī khalqi al-samawati wal-arḍi…* These are the verses that meditate on the signs of Allah ﷻ in the heavens and the earth, the alternation of night and day. The Prophet ﷺ greeted his morning with them.

## Hands before water

Before stepping into wudu, the Prophet ﷺ taught a small protective practice for the body that had been at rest.

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: «إِذَا اسْتَيْقَظَ أَحَدُكُمْ مِنْ نَوْمِهِ، فَلْيَغْسِلْ يَدَهُ قَبْلَ أَنْ يُدْخِلَهَا فِي وَضُوئِهِ، فَإِنَّ أَحَدَكُمْ لَا يَدْرِي أَيْنَ بَاتَتْ يَدُهُ»
>
> **English:** *"When one of you wakes from sleep, let him wash his hand before putting it into his wudu water, for none of you knows where his hand spent the night."*
>
> *— Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 162; Sahih Muslim, no. 278. See HID-0057. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

The reasoning is concrete: *the hand has been moving while you slept; you do not know where it has been.* The teaching is small and practical, the kind of thing a careful host might say to a guest. Wash the hand before it enters the water you will use to purify yourself.

## What these small acts undo

The earlier sections describe *what* the Prophet ﷺ did on waking. This narration explains *why* the small acts matter.

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: «يَعْقِدُ الشَّيْطَانُ عَلَى قَافِيَةِ رَأْسِ أَحَدِكُمْ إِذَا هُوَ نَامَ ثَلَاثَ عُقَدٍ، يَضْرِبُ كُلَّ عُقْدَةٍ: عَلَيْكَ لَيْلٌ طَوِيلٌ فَارْقُدْ. فَإِنِ اسْتَيْقَظَ فَذَكَرَ اللَّهَ انْحَلَّتْ عُقْدَةٌ، فَإِنْ تَوَضَّأَ انْحَلَّتْ عُقْدَةٌ، فَإِنْ صَلَّى انْحَلَّتْ عُقَدُهُ كُلُّهَا، فَأَصْبَحَ نَشِيطًا طَيِّبَ النَّفْسِ، وَإِلَّا أَصْبَحَ خَبِيثَ النَّفْسِ كَسْلَانَ»
>
> **English:** *"When one of you sleeps, Satan ties three knots at the back of his head, striking each knot, saying: 'You have a long night, so sleep on.' If he wakes and remembers Allah, one knot is untied. If he performs wudu, another knot is untied. If he prays, all the knots are untied — and he begins his morning energetic, good of soul. If not, he begins his morning ill of soul, sluggish."*
>
> *— Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 1142; Sahih Muslim, no. 776. See HID-0058. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

The hadith names three knots and three corresponding acts — *dhikr*, *wudu*, *salah*. Each acts like the loosening of a single binding. By the time the Prophet ﷺ had stood for prayer, all three were undone. The narration ties together every other waking practice in this chapter: the dua is dhikr; the hand-washing belongs to wudu; the recitation of *Aal Imran* often led, in his ﷺ practice, into night prayer or Fajr. None of the small acts is a small act on its own.

---

## Practical lessons

- The Prophet ﷺ greeted his ﷺ first moment of waking with praise: *Alhamdulillah ladhi ahyana…* (HID-0046).
- He ﷺ wiped sleep from his ﷺ face and recited the last ten verses of *Surah Aal Imran* (HID-0056).
- He ﷺ taught washing the hand before placing it in the wudu water (HID-0057).
- He ﷺ described three knots loosened by *dhikr*, *wudu*, and *salah* — a morning begun with these is energetic and good of soul (HID-0058).
- The miswak followed on rising at night (see HID-0003 in *Miswak and Cleanliness*).

The morning, in the way the Prophet ﷺ lived it, is built from the smallest acts. The eyes open. The hand wipes the face. The tongue forms a sentence of praise. The verses are recited. The hand is washed. The wudu begins. And by the time the first light of Fajr arrives, the day has already been entered with care.
