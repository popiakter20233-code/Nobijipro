# Wudu and Preparation for Salah

> *Draft status:* MEDIUM confidence. Wudu sections (HID-0008..0010) are in **Batch 1**. Masjid and Jumu'ah sections (HID-0040..0051) sit in a later batch and carry additional FIQH and TRANSLATION DEFERRED concentrations.

---

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ did not approach *Salah* abruptly. He ﷺ prepared. He ﷺ walked. He ﷺ greeted the threshold. The Sahabah رضي الله عنهم who travelled the path with him watched and remembered three layers of the same gesture — the body's preparation in *wudu*, the small etiquettes of arriving at the masjid, and the weekly discipline of *Jumu'ah*. This chapter walks through them in order.

---

# Part 1 — Wudu

The wudu was the entryway. The Prophet ﷺ taught both its *form* — the careful, sequenced washing — and its *meaning* — that without it, *Salah* is not received. But before the wudu came another small movement of the day: the visit to the place of relief, framed at both ends by remembrance.

## Before wudu — toilet etiquette

### A — Direct hadith evidence

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَنَسٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: كَانَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ إِذَا دَخَلَ الْخَلَاءَ قَالَ: «اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْخُبُثِ وَالْخَبَائِثِ»
>
> **English:** *"When the Prophet ﷺ would enter the bathroom, he ﷺ would say: 'O Allah, I seek refuge in You from al-khub(u)thi wal-khaba'ith.'"*
>
> *— Anas ibn Malik رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 142; Sahih Muslim, no. 375 (muttafaq alayh). See HID-0054. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED] [TRANSLATION REVIEW]*

The dua names what is sought refuge from — *al-khubth* (impurity, filth) and *al-khaba'ith* (evil things). Some classical readers vocalize the first word as *al-khubuth* — male devils — paired with *al-khaba'ith* as female devils. Both readings are classical; the chapter retains the Arabic and defers the rendering to the scholar.

> **Arabic:** عَنْ عَائِشَةَ رضي الله عنها قَالَتْ: «كَانَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ إِذَا خَرَجَ مِنَ الْخَلَاءِ قَالَ: غُفْرَانَكَ»
>
> **English:** *"When the Prophet ﷺ would come out of the bathroom, he ﷺ would say: 'Ghufrānak — Your forgiveness, O Allah.'"*
>
> *— Aishah رضي الله عنها. Sunan Abu Dawud, no. 30; Jami` at-Tirmidhi, no. 7. Graded sahih (al-Albani). See HID-0055. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

The leaving-dua is a single word: *ghufrānak*, "Your forgiveness." Scholars have noted its appropriateness in different ways — gratitude that the burden was relieved without difficulty, awareness of the moments unable to be spent in dhikr, humility in returning from a place of human frailty. The Prophet ﷺ does not name the reason; he ﷺ simply asks.

### B — Scholarly / practical application

The duas (HID-0054 and HID-0055) apply directly to entering and leaving any place of relief — fixed toilet, traveler's stop, modern bathroom. The wording does not change with the place.

The widespread teaching that **the left foot enters and the right foot leaves** the toilet is **a practical application of the broader Sunnah principle that the right takes precedence in clean and noble matters** (HID-0052). It is consistent with the principle and aligns with the sandals hadith (HID-0053). It is not, however, named in a directly-authenticated hadith with the same Sahihayn-grade strength as the duas. The chapter presents it as practical inference, clearly labeled.

### C — Modern application

Modern bathrooms — fitted toilets, attached washrooms, public facilities — are covered by the same duas; the change in architecture does not change the practice. The duas are said before crossing the threshold and after leaving, as the Prophet ﷺ said them.

## The form: washing each limb three times

> **Arabic:** عَنْ عُثْمَانَ بْنِ عَفَّانَ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ تَوَضَّأَ ثَلَاثًا ثَلَاثًا
>
> **English:** *"The Prophet ﷺ performed wudu, washing each limb three times."*
>
> *— Narrated by Uthman ibn Affan رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 159; Sahih Muslim, no. 226 (muttafaq alayh). See HID-0008. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED] [NAME PROVISIONAL] [FIQH REVIEW]*

The fuller narration in the source describes the entire sequence in detail. The condensation here records the cardinal point: each limb washed three times. The three-fold washing is presented in the hadith as the Prophet's ﷺ settled practice. Madhab discussions on minimum and maximum number of washes are deferred to scholar treatment.

---

## The meaning: no Salah without purification

> **Arabic:** عَنِ ابْنِ عُمَرَ رضي الله عنهما عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: «لَا تُقْبَلُ صَلَاةٌ بِغَيْرِ طُهُورٍ»
>
> **English:** *"No Salah is accepted without purification."*
>
> *— Narrated by Abdullah ibn Umar رضي الله عنهما. Sahih Muslim, no. 224. See HID-0009. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED] [NAME PROVISIONAL] [FIQH REVIEW]*

The Prophet ﷺ states the relationship between *taharah* and *Salah* directly. The wudu is not an addition to the prayer; it is the prayer's gateway.

---

## The grace: perfect wudu erases sins

> **Arabic:** عَنْ عُثْمَانَ بْنِ عَفَّانَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللهِ ﷺ: «مَنْ تَوَضَّأَ فَأَحْسَنَ الْوُضُوءَ خَرَجَتْ خَطَايَاهُ مِنْ جَسَدِهِ حَتَّى تَخْرُجَ مِنْ تَحْتِ أَظْفَارِهِ»
>
> **English:** *"Whoever performs wudu well, his sins exit from his body — even from beneath his fingernails."*
>
> *— Narrated by Uthman ibn Affan رضي الله عنه. Sahih Muslim, no. 245. See HID-0010. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED] [NAME PROVISIONAL]*

The Prophet ﷺ describes wudu's effect not just as ritual cleanliness but as a removal of sins. The phrase *aḥsana al-wudū'a* — "performed wudu well" — invites attention to *quality*, not merely the act. The verb *aḥsana* means more than completion; it means doing it beautifully, with care.

# Part 2 — Visiting the Masjid

After wudu came the walk. Even the journey to the masjid was, the Prophet ﷺ said, valued — and at the threshold, three small acts shaped the entry.

---

## Going to the masjid — a place prepared

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: «مَنْ غَدَا إِلَى الْمَسْجِدِ وَرَاحَ، أَعَدَّ اللَّهُ لَهُ نُزُلَهُ مِنَ الْجَنَّةِ كُلَّمَا غَدَا أَوْ رَاحَ»
>
> **English:** *"Whoever goes to the masjid in the morning or evening, Allah ﷻ prepares for him a place of welcome in Paradise each time he goes."*
>
> *— Narrated by Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه. Sahih Muslim, no. 666. See HID-0040. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

The Prophet ﷺ frames the journey as already-counted hospitality. Each return to the masjid is met, somewhere ahead, with a place prepared in welcome.

---

## At the threshold — paired duas

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَبِي حُمَيْدٍ، أَوْ عَنْ أَبِي أُسَيْدٍ رضي الله عنهما قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: «إِذَا دَخَلَ أَحَدُكُمُ الْمَسْجِدَ فَلْيَقُلْ: اللَّهُمَّ افْتَحْ لِي أَبْوَابَ رَحْمَتِكَ. وَإِذَا خَرَجَ فَلْيَقُلْ: اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْأَلُكَ مِنْ فَضْلِكَ»
>
> **English:** *"When one of you enters the masjid, let him say: 'O Allah, open the doors of Your mercy for me.' And when he leaves, let him say: 'O Allah, I ask You from Your bounty.'"*
>
> *— Narrated by Abu Humayd or Abu Usayd رضي الله عنهما. Sahih Muslim, no. 713. See HID-0041. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED] [NAME PROVISIONAL]*

The two duas are paired by direction: entering, *open the doors of mercy*; leaving, *I ask from Your bounty*. The masjid is named as the place of mercy; the world outside, the place where bounty is sought.

---

## Two raka'at before sitting — Tahiyyat al-Masjid

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَبِي قَتَادَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: «إِذَا دَخَلَ أَحَدُكُمُ الْمَسْجِدَ فَلَا يَجْلِسْ حَتَّى يُصَلِّيَ رَكْعَتَيْنِ»
>
> **English:** *"When one of you enters the masjid, let him not sit until he has prayed two raka'at."*
>
> *— Narrated by Abu Qatadah رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 1167; Sahih Muslim, no. 714 (muttafaq alayh). See HID-0042. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED] [NAME PROVISIONAL] [FIQH REVIEW]*

The Prophet ﷺ taught a specific gesture for the masjid itself — two raka'at greeting the building. The masjid's hospitality is met with the visitor's offering.

# Part 3 — Jumu'ah Routine

Beyond the daily prayers, Friday gathered the community. Three narrations describe what the Prophet ﷺ named about it: the body's preparation, the listening that makes the gathering valuable, and the hour when prayer is heard.

---

## The body's preparation — Friday ghusl

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: «غُسْلُ يَوْمِ الْجُمُعَةِ وَاجِبٌ عَلَى كُلِّ مُحْتَلِمٍ»
>
> **English:** *"Ghusl on Friday is **wājib** on every adult."*  *(The term **wājib** is preserved in transliteration. Its juristic reading varies across madhabs — lexically "binding"; juristically read variously as obligatory, or as strongly emphasized sunnah. The English and Bangla wording is deferred to the scholar; see scholar packet.)*
>
> *— Narrated by Abu Sa'id al-Khudri رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 877; Sahih Muslim, no. 846 (muttafaq alayh). See HID-0049. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED] [NAME PROVISIONAL] [FIQH REVIEW] [TRANSLATION REVIEW] [TRANSLATION DEFERRED — *wājib*]*

The chapter does not commit to a translation of *wājib* in the body text. The term carries weight that a casual rendering would lose; the scholar selects the final wording, with a brief footnote on madhab differences if helpful.

---

## Listening attentively — even silence is shaped

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: «إِذَا قُلْتَ لِصَاحِبِكَ يَوْمَ الْجُمُعَةِ: أَنْصِتْ، وَالْإِمَامُ يَخْطُبُ فَقَدْ لَغَوْتَ»
>
> **English:** *"If you say to your companion on Friday, 'Be quiet,' while the imam is delivering the khutbah, you have spoken vainly."*
>
> *— Narrated by Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 934; Sahih Muslim, no. 851 (muttafaq alayh). See HID-0050. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

The Prophet ﷺ describes a paradox: even an attempt to silence another during the khutbah is itself a form of *laghw* — vain speech. The standard the Prophet ﷺ sets is severe and serious: attention is not optional in the Friday gathering.

---

## The hour of acceptance

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ ذَكَرَ يَوْمَ الْجُمُعَةِ فَقَالَ: «فِيهِ سَاعَةٌ لَا يُوَافِقُهَا عَبْدٌ مُسْلِمٌ وَهُوَ قَائِمٌ يُصَلِّي يَسْأَلُ اللَّهَ شَيْئًا إِلَّا أَعْطَاهُ إِيَّاهُ»
>
> **English:** *"(The Prophet ﷺ) mentioned Friday and said: 'In it is an hour during which no Muslim servant standing in prayer asks Allah ﷻ for anything but that He gives it to him.'"*
>
> *— Narrated by Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 935; Sahih Muslim, no. 852 (muttafaq alayh). See HID-0051. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

The Prophet ﷺ names the existence of an hour without fixing its boundaries. Scholarly opinions vary on its exact timing — some after Asr, some at the end of the day, some during the khutbah. The chapter retains the description without entering the timing-debate.

## Practical lessons

- The Prophet ﷺ said *Allahumma inni a'udhu bika min al-khub(u)thi wal-khaba'ith* on entering the bathroom (HID-0054).
- He ﷺ said *Ghufrānak* on leaving the bathroom (HID-0055).
- *(Practical application:)* the left foot enters and the right foot leaves the toilet — a practical extension of the right-side principle (HID-0052), not a directly stated hadith.
- The Prophet ﷺ washed each limb three times in wudu (HID-0008).
- *Taharah* is the prerequisite of accepted *Salah* (HID-0009).
- Performing wudu well is described as causing one's sins to exit one's body (HID-0010).
- Each journey to the masjid is met with a place prepared in welcome (HID-0040).
- The Prophet ﷺ taught paired duas — *open the doors of mercy* on entering, *I ask from Your bounty* on leaving (HID-0041).
- He ﷺ taught praying two raka'at before sitting on entering the masjid (HID-0042).
- He ﷺ named ghusl on Friday with the term *wājib* (translation deferred to scholar; HID-0049).
- He ﷺ taught that even silencing another during the khutbah is itself laghw (HID-0050).
- He ﷺ named a Friday hour during which Allah ﷻ answers a Muslim servant's prayer (HID-0051).

The chapter walks the same path the Sahabah walked beside the Prophet ﷺ: the body's preparation in *wudu*, the journey to the masjid, the weekly assembly. None of the three layers stands without the others. The chapter holds them together in the order in which the Prophet ﷺ lived them.
