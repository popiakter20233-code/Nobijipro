# Miswak and Cleanliness

> *Draft status:* MEDIUM confidence. Every cited hadith carries `[NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW]` and `[HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]` until verified against published editions. Chapter is in `drafts/`, not yet promoted to `verified/`. **Batch 1** — first scholar-review batch.

---

Among the practices the Sahabah رضي الله عنهم preserved with extraordinary care was something so small it could be missed: a tooth-stick. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ kept a *miswak* close. He ﷺ reached for it on entering his ﷺ home, on rising at night, and as a part of his ﷺ preparation for *Salah*. He ﷺ told his Companions, in words they remembered: *were it not difficult for them, he ﷺ would have made it obligatory*.

What follows are four narrations on his ﷺ relationship with the miswak. They come from *Sahih al-Bukhari*, *Sahih Muslim*, and *Sunan an-Nasa'i*. Together they describe a settled habit — small in itself, large in what it suggests.

---

## A practice he ﷺ wished to mandate

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللهِ ﷺ قَالَ: «لَوْلَا أَنْ أَشُقَّ عَلَى أُمَّتِي لَأَمَرْتُهُمْ بِالسِّوَاكِ مَعَ كُلِّ صَلَاةٍ»
>
> **English:** *"Were it not that it would cause hardship for my Ummah, I would have ordered them to use the miswak before every Salah."*
>
> *— Narrated by Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 887; Sahih Muslim, no. 252 (muttafaq alayh). See HID-0001. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

Of all the narrations on oral cleanliness in the hadith corpus, this is among the most cited — and worth pausing over. The Prophet ﷺ does not say, *I taught it.* He says, *I would have ordered it, but for the burden it would have placed on you.* The practice, then, is something he ﷺ loved — loved enough to consider obligating, loved enough to refrain from obligating. The mercy is in the restraint.

---

## What he ﷺ did first on entering his home

> **Arabic:** عَنْ عَائِشَةَ رضي الله عنها قَالَتْ: «كَانَ رَسُولُ اللهِ ﷺ إِذَا دَخَلَ بَيْتَهُ بَدَأَ بِالسِّوَاكِ»
>
> **English:** *"When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ entered his house, the first thing he would do was use the miswak."*
>
> *— Narrated by Aishah رضي الله عنها. Sahih Muslim, no. 253. See HID-0002. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

Aishah رضي الله عنها knew the rhythms of his ﷺ household better than anyone. She does not describe an occasional act; she uses the verbal form *kāna* — *he used to* — the form Arabic reserves for habit. The miswak preceded greetings, preceded sitting, preceded the meal. It was the threshold of his ﷺ time at home.

---

## What he ﷺ did first on rising at night

> **Arabic:** عَنْ حُذَيْفَةَ بْنِ الْيَمَانِ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: «كَانَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ إِذَا قَامَ مِنَ اللَّيْلِ يَشُوصُ فَاهُ بِالسِّوَاكِ»
>
> **English:** *"When the Prophet ﷺ rose at night, he would clean his mouth thoroughly with the miswak."*
>
> *— Narrated by Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 245; Sahih Muslim, no. 255 (muttafaq alayh). See HID-0003. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED] [NAME PROVISIONAL]*

The Arabic verb *yashūṣu* is forceful — *to scrub thoroughly*. Hudhayfah رضي الله عنه describes a Prophet ﷺ who took the time, in the quiet of the night, to clean his ﷺ mouth before standing in *Tahajjud*. The picture is small but precise: the miswak in his ﷺ hand, the bed left behind, the prayer ahead.

## A means of purity, a means of pleasing the Lord

> **Arabic:** عَنْ عَائِشَةَ رضي الله عنها عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: «السِّوَاكُ مَطْهَرَةٌ لِلْفَمِ مَرْضَاةٌ لِلرَّبِّ»
>
> **English:** *"The miswak is a means of purification for the mouth and a means of pleasing the Lord."*
>
> *— Narrated by Aishah رضي الله عنها. Sunan an-Nasa'i, no. 5; also Musnad Ahmad and Sahih Ibn Khuzaymah; graded sahih by al-Albani. See HID-0004. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED] [TRANSLATION REVIEW]*

The phrasing is striking. The Prophet ﷺ pairs two effects of a single small act: a physical effect — *maṭharah*, a means of cleansing — and a spiritual one — *marḍātun lir-Rabb*, a means of pleasing the Lord ﷻ. The clause does not promise reward in some far Hereafter; it locates the reward in the act itself. The mouth that opens for the *Salah* is a mouth that has been prepared. *(The exact translator-approved English and Bangla wording of "marḍātun lir-Rabb" should be confirmed by the scholar before publication.)*

## Grooming — the five practices of *fitrah*

Beyond the miswak, the Prophet ﷺ named five small practices that belong to the human being's *fitrah* — the natural, original constitution. They are practical, repeatable, and unglamorous; together they shape the body's basic readiness.

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: «الْفِطْرَةُ خَمْسٌ: الْخِتَانُ، وَالاسْتِحْدَادُ، وَقَصُّ الشَّارِبِ، وَتَقْلِيمُ الْأَظْفَارِ، وَنَتْفُ الْإِبِطِ»
>
> **English:** *"Fitrah consists of five: circumcision, removing the pubic hair, trimming the moustache, clipping the nails, and plucking the underarm hair."*
>
> *— Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 5891; Sahih Muslim, no. 257 (muttafaq alayh). See HID-0059. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

The list is not exhortation; it is description. The Prophet ﷺ names five things and stops. He ﷺ does not explain at length, does not rank, does not threaten. The five are simply named as belonging to the original way a human is meant to be cared for.

## Hair, oil, and care

The Prophet ﷺ did not neglect his ﷺ hair, nor did he allow others to neglect theirs. Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه preserved a short, sharp instruction:

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: «مَنْ كَانَ لَهُ شَعْرٌ فَلْيُكْرِمْهُ»
>
> **English:** *"Whoever has hair, let him honour it."*
>
> *— Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه. Sunan Abu Dawud, no. 4163; graded hasan by al-Albani. See HID-0060. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

The Arabic verb *yukrim* — "honour" — is the same word used for honouring guests, honouring parents, honouring the Qur'an. It is a respectful verb; the hair on one's head is named as something worthy of that respect. Combing it, oiling it, keeping it from becoming unkempt are not vanity — they are courtesy to the body itself.

The same general principle of *right-side preference* applies here: the Prophet ﷺ loved beginning with the right side in his ﷺ shoes, in his ﷺ combing of hair, in his ﷺ wudu, and in all his affairs (see HID-0052 in *Clothing and Appearance*).

## Perfume — what he ﷺ would not refuse

The Prophet ﷺ welcomed good scent. Anas ibn Malik رضي الله عنه, who lived in the household and watched daily life closely, preserved one short habit:

> **Arabic:** عَنْ أَنَسٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: «كَانَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ لَا يَرُدُّ الطِّيبَ»
>
> **English:** *"The Prophet ﷺ would not refuse perfume."*
>
> *— Anas ibn Malik رضي الله عنه. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 2582; Sahih Muslim, no. 2330. See HID-0061. [NEEDS SCHOLAR REVIEW] [HUMAN REVIEW RECOMMENDED]*

Anas رضي الله عنه states it as a negation — *the Prophet ﷺ did not refuse perfume*. The phrasing is gentle. He ﷺ did not perform a ritual around perfume; he ﷺ simply accepted it when offered, and used it. *Ṭīb* in the hadith is the broad word for good fragrance — oil, attar, scent of any clean kind.

## Practical lessons

These observations are *descriptive* of the Prophet's ﷺ practice; the reader takes from them what the reader will.

- The Prophet ﷺ used the miswak before *Salah* (HID-0001).
- He ﷺ used the miswak on entering his ﷺ home (HID-0002).
- He ﷺ used the miswak on rising at night, before *Tahajjud* (HID-0003).
- He ﷺ described the miswak as both physically purifying and spiritually meritorious (HID-0004).
- He ﷺ named five practices of *fitrah*: circumcision, removing the pubic hair, trimming the moustache, clipping the nails, plucking the underarm hair (HID-0059).
- He ﷺ taught that one who has hair should honour it — care for it, comb it (HID-0060).
- He ﷺ did not refuse good fragrance when it was offered (HID-0061).

Among the small habits the Sahabah رضي الله عنهم preserved, this one returns again and again. What they remembered, they passed on; what they passed on, this chapter passes on in turn. The miswak is a small thing. The care he ﷺ gave it is a larger thing. The connection between the two is the chapter's quiet point.
